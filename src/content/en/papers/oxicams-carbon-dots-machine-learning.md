## Research background

Oxicams are a common class of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs. Sensitive detection and discrimination support pharmaceutical analysis and risk monitoring. This study combines fluorescence sensing with machine learning rather than relying on a single spectral reading.

## Sensing method

The study prepares fluorine- and nitrogen-codoped carbon dots through a hydrothermal method. The material has two excitation peaks near 280 nm and 340 nm. At a fixed emission wavelength, the ratio of the two excitation intensities changes with oxicam concentration. Meloxicam serves as the model compound for validating quantitative detection.

## Machine learning for discrimination

After comparing several algorithms, the study uses XGBoost to distinguish oxicam drugs at low concentrations. It also develops a convolutional-neural-network-assisted sensing platform for predicting concentrations in real samples.

According to the abstract in indexing metadata, the method has a detection limit of 97 nM for meloxicam over a concentration range of 0.097–25 μM. XGBoost achieves 100% accuracy on unknown samples within the reported experimental dataset. These are the paper’s reported results and should not be generalized beyond the original experimental setting.
